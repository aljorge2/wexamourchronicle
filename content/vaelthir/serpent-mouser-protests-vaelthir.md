Protests over Operation MantiMouser spread through Vaelthir this week, as the serpent-mouser patrols that came up the south road with the Bureau's officers more than a week ago went on patrolling with no word from the office on what they are there to hunt. What began a fortnight ago as a few members of the Wexamour Archaeological Society standing outside the Office of Resource and Civil Management's Vaelthir station, at the call of their president Edna Thatch, had grown by Friday evening into a crowd that filled the street from the station steps to the edge of the University square. There were students from the University, farmers up from the Downs by cart, and serpent-folk families who said they no longer felt safe on their own roads. They carried lanterns, and banners painted with the words Neighbours, Not Prey, and as the light went they took up a single chant toward the station doors and kept it up for hours: "Call off the mousers." Near the end of the evening at least four of the office's own clerks came out through those doors and stood on the step with them.

Bureau officers lined the steps throughout, and no arrests had been reported by the time this paper went to print. An ORCM spokesperson told the Chronicle that the program remains "a routine, precautionary measure for the safety of our personnel and posts," and declined to say whether the patrols would be called back, what threat they were deployed against, or who answers for the animals once they are loosed on the roads. The protesters say they will be back on the steps every evening until the serpent-mousers are pulled off the strip. "They have put animals bred to kill snakes on the roads my children walk home on," a serpent-folk woman from the strip told me, holding her lantern up toward the doors. "And they will not even tell us why."

*Merritt Vance is a staff reporter for the Chronicle's Vaelthir section. Anyone who has attended the protests, or who has been stopped by the patrols on the Faded Lands roads, is urged to write to the Vaelthir desk by owl. Correspondence will be held in confidence.*
