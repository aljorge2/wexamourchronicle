I should say at the top that Godfrey Ashmere suspended me from this paper, killed four of my pieces, and gave my notebook to the Bureau. I did not like him and I am not going to pretend I did. I am going to report his death the way I would report anyone's, which is the way he would not report mine.

## What happened

Godfrey Ashmere was found dead in the study of his house on Cressmere Lane at first light on Day 236 by his housekeeper, Mrs. Agathe Primm, who has kept the house for eleven years. She told me the study door was shut but not locked, the lamp on his desk had burned down to nothing, and he was in his chair. She did not touch him. She went straight out into the lane and called for help.

The Bureau was at the house within the hour. By noon the body had been taken away and the house was sealed. The Office has not said how he died, where he has been taken, or when his family may have him. I asked all three. I was told the matter is in hand.

## What the Office says

The Office has put out a statement, and it ran on the wire desk this morning before the ward broke. It calls his death "a cowardly act by persons associated with the Archaeological Society, who for a fortnight have threatened Mr. Ashmere in the pages they stole from him." It asks anyone who knows a member of the Society to come forward at the Vaelthir station.

The people who hold the ward have told me they did not kill him. They have also told me to print everything I found that points at them, and not to leave any of it out. I have not.

## The first thing

When I got to Cressmere Lane the Bureau had already put a line across the gate, so I saw the front door from the street. Someone has cut a Claddagh into it, deep, the width of a hand: two hands, a heart, a crown. It was not there when the house was last painted. Mrs. Primm says it was not there when she locked up the night before.

She also told me that on his desk, set square in the middle of the blotter, was a page torn out of last week's Chronicle. It was folded once. It was the page that said GODFREY ASHMERE IS A TYRANT.

I will not tell you that means nothing. It is the mark the Society wears, cut into the door of a man the Society had every reason to hate, and the Society's own words left where he would have been sitting.

## The second thing

The people who hold the ward still had his mailbox. They have had it since they took the messages they printed in these pages last week, and they were still reading it on the evening of Day 235.

At dusk that evening a message came in from Lysander Pryce, of the Office's public affairs desk. They showed it to me. This is all of it:

> The Chair would like a word tonight.

Some time before dawn, every message in Godfrey Ashmere's mailbox was deleted. Not some of them. Every one, back to the day he took the chair. The record of the session that did it shows it was opened from an address ending in orcm.gov.wx. I took that record to the clerk who keeps the Chronicle's own mail, and she told me it was not opened from anywhere in this building.

I wrote to Mr. Pryce and asked whether he or anyone from the Office saw Mr. Ashmere on the night he died. His desk replied that the Office "does not comment on the private correspondence of private persons."

## What I can tell you

A carving can be cut by anyone with a knife. A page can be left on a desk by anyone with a key, or anyone the door was opened for. A mailbox can be emptied by anyone the Office has given its word for the door. None of what I have proves who killed him. All of it proves that somebody wants you to think they know.

I do not know who killed Godfrey Ashmere. I will not pretend that I do, and I would ask the Office to stop pretending that it does until it can tell his family how he died.

He told me once, in writing, that he would rather be wrong beside the Office than right beside a crowd. I do not know which of them was beside him at the end.

*Merritt Vance reports for the Wexamour Archaeological Society. He wrote for the Chronicle's Vaelthir pages until its editor suspended him. If you saw anyone on Cressmere Lane on the night of Day 235, write to him by owl. Correspondence will be held in confidence, and this time it will be kept.*
