For three weeks the families of the people killed on the square the night of the vigil were told to apply at the station, in person, in daylight. They applied. They were told the matter was in hand. The Office told this paper there would be no figure for the square and that the wagons were finished before dawn. It did not say where they had finished.

On Day 239, the families went and found out.

## How they knew where to look

One of the clerks who walked out of the Vaelthir station to stand with the crowd last month remembered a line in the yard book from the morning of Day 220. Three wagons, out at the Fifth Hour, marked *sent south*. Nothing else. No place, no name, and no wagons marked back in.

South of the city there is one road that matters, and since the curfew it has been closed past the second creek, at every hour, with officers at the crossing to turn people back. The Office has never said why. Most of the strip decided some time ago that they knew.

The crossing was still guarded on the night of Day 239, so the families did not use it. They went the way Hollis Barrow wrote about in these pages some weeks ago, by the single track that runs south from the creek and is on no map, the one the strip has spent its whole life not talking about. There were forty of us, give or take, and three handcarts, and a lantern we did not light until we were past the trees.

## What we found

A little over a mile past the crossing, the road drops into a hollow on its west side. The three wagons were down in it, pulled off the road and left. The horses had been taken out of the shafts and led away. No one had buried anyone. The dead were still lying where they had been laid in the wagon beds, under the Society's banners, the ones that read Neighbours, Not Prey, which their neighbours had put over them under the University wall.

The banners had kept the weather off them. I am not going to say more about it than that. The families do not want it written down, and they have earned the right to decide.

They were left the way you leave something you mean to come back for, and then do not.

## Bringing them home

We loaded them onto the handcarts and the wagons both, since the wagons were ours to take by then, and we pulled them north by hand. It took the rest of the night. Nobody came down the road after us. When we came up past the crossing from the wrong side at first light, the two officers posted there watched us go by and did not say a word, and one of them took off his cap.

Before we left the hollow, I looked back south once. The road keeps going past it, up onto high ground, and at the top, where there is nothing on any map I have seen, a light was burning. Not a lantern. Something steadier than that. I asked the people near me what it was. Nobody would say.

## Day 240

They were buried on the strip and in the Downs and in the city on Day 240, each in their own family's ground, each under the banner they came home in. It was after dusk before the last of them was in. The curfew was not kept that night, not by anyone, in any of the three places. Lanterns were lit on every road between them. Nobody was arrested for it.

## The thirty

I said I had twenty-six of the thirty names and was still looking for the other four. The families gave me the last four on the road. I have marked them, because they waited longest.

**Cut down with Bureau swords, at the foot of the steps and across the square: fourteen.**

- Marta Coombes, 33, Pip's mother, who had set Pip's lantern by the doors within the hour.
- Sela Wick, 49, Ivy's mother.
- Gethin Byre, 70, father of Denner and grandfather of Ossie.
- Rowan Attlebury, 29, Hester's son.
- Dorrit Castellan, 44, Wynn's mother.
- Abel Critch, 41, Tobin's father.
- Brannoc Kettleby, 70, Maudie's husband.
- Teague Orrin, 37, Sable's husband.
- Maddox Denby, 25, Harlan's son.
- Peregrine Thresher, 31, Alma's grandson.
- Corin Marsh, 40, Isolde's husband.
- Fenwick Hale, 30, Prewitt's son.
- Tamsin Ambry, 45, Nils's wife.
- Jory Pike, 19, Wendel's grandson.

**Crushed against the University gates: nine.**

- Isaura Penhallow, 20, a student of the University.
- Bastian Quill, 21, a student of the University.
- Hob Fairweather, 52, who farmed outside Millhaven and came the long way round when the carts were turned back at the Brannock bridge.
- Clemency Arden, 34.
- Rufus Holloway, 60.
- Della Brisk, 27.
- Willa Hart, 38.
- Nell Garrow, 17. *(One of the four.)*
- Osric Penn, 45. *(One of the four.)*

**Killed by the Bureau's serpent-mousers: four.**

- Silas Venn, 12, who was being arrested when the mouser was let off its lead.
- Mirren Hask, 55.
- Thane Ossory, 40.
- Calla Strand, 29. *(One of the four.)*

**Burned when the lanterns thrown from the top of the steps set the banners alight: three.**

- Ewan Lockwood, 66.
- Hamish Crane, 50.
- Briony Teal, 24. *(One of the four.)*

That is thirty. The Office has still not counted one of them. It does not need to anymore.
