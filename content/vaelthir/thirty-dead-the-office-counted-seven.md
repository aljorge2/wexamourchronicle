The office has told you that seven of its officers were hurt on the steps of the Vaelthir station, and it has told you how, one by one: burns to an officer's hands, neck, and face; an officer who fell the length of the steps and broke his wrist; a head wound from a thrown stone; a bite, and treatment for venom; and three more with sprains, cuts, and heavy bruising "consistent with a sustained assault." It has told you that among the crowd "a small number of persons reported minor complaints," and that all of them were discharged the same night.

Here is the small number.

## Thirty dead

I counted them under the University wall, where their neighbours laid them out under the Society's banners, before the Bureau's wagons came onto the square and took them away. I have the names of twenty-six. I am still looking for the other four.

**Cut down with Bureau swords, at the foot of the station steps and across the square: fourteen.** Most of them were family of the twenty whose names Mrs. Thatch was reading. They had just carried those lanterns up the steps.

- Sela, 49
- Bess Thornby, 45
- Hanna Byre, 41
- Nettie Combe, 50
- Corin Castellan, 44
- Jem Castellan, 14
- Aldwin Marsh, 36
- Gideon Attlebury, 55
- Perrin Down, 9
- Marla Hale, 56
- Agatha Fairweather, 67
- Two I could not name.

**Crushed against the University gates, which were shut behind the crowd while the names were being read: nine.**

- Odette Marrow, 52
- Maud Pellingham, 61
- Jory Millward, 38
- Thaddeus Ashe, 58
- Aurelie Penn, 19
- Tamsin Vey, 20
- Bram Whitlow, 63
- Cressida Lowe, 11
- One I could not name.

**Killed by the Bureau's serpent-mousers after their handlers let them off the lead: four.** All four were serpent-folk. One was the boy of twelve who was being arrested by the officer the office tells you was bitten. The officer was given the treatment for the venom. The boy was not.

- Wil Critch, 12
- Dov Kettleby, 40
- Ines Ambry, 44
- Rook Harrow, 8

**Burned when the lanterns thrown from the top of the steps set the banners alight in the crowd: three.**

- Ewan Orrin, 37
- Fenna Rourke, 35
- One I could not name.

That is thirty. The office has not counted one of them.

## Twenty who may not live the week

Twenty more were carried to the waystation infirmary with wounds I do not expect all of them to survive. Twelve have sword wounds. Five were pulled out of the crush at the gates and have not woken. Two are burned over most of their bodies. One is a serpent-folk woman bitten by a mouser. Among the twenty are two members of the Society: a woman of seventy-one who was carried in on a door, and the woman who beat out the fire on Officer Wren's collar with her shawl and was struck down for it.

## Fifty hurt the way the seven were hurt

The office listed the injuries of its seven officers one by one. Fifty people from the crowd were hurt in exactly the same ways, and nobody has listed them. I have set them out below in the same words the office used for its officers.

- Nine with burns to the hands, neck, and face.
- Eleven who fell, or were thrown, the length of the steps, with broken wrists and arms.
- Fourteen with head wounds, from batons and from the hilts of swords.
- Three who were bitten and are being treated for possible venom.
- Thirteen with sprains, cuts, and heavy bruising consistent with a sustained assault.

That is fifty, and every one of them is an injury the office thought grave enough to list when it happened to an officer, but not when it happened to a citizen.

## How they were written down

The clerk on the infirmary desk wrote down every one of them as they came in: the name if there was one, the wound, and the hour. She showed me the ledger herself, so that someone outside that building would know it existed.

Shortly after the Third Hour, a Bureau officer brought a note from the office: the beds were to be kept clear for officers. By the Fifth Hour the infirmary had sent home everyone who could be moved, and a good many who could not, the twenty I counted among them. The woman of seventy-one went home in her grandson's handcart.

At the Fifth Hour, the officers took the ledger from the desk, and they took the clerk with it. I watched them walk her out. By morning the office was telling you there had been a small number of minor complaints.

That night the infirmary kept one patient: the officer with the head wound. Every other bed was empty.

## What I could not count

I could not count the eleven who were taken at the front, because they were taken inside the station and not to the infirmary, and the office has not said whether any of them were hurt. Mrs. Edna Thatch, who is sixty-two, is among them. I could not count the people carried straight home by their neighbours because they would not go anywhere an officer might be waiting. I cannot tell you where the infirmary clerk was taken. I have asked the office where the eleven are being held, where the clerk is, where the wagons took the thirty, and whether their families may have them back. I have not been answered.

Thirty dead, twenty dying, fifty hurt. One hundred people against the office's seven. That is the small number.
