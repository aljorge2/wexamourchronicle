I am not a writer, and this paper has told me twice that I may not write for it. I am writing anyway, because nobody else alive knew them both.

## Ikavelth

For two years Ikavelth Lugrathe came to my desk at the archive with more questions than the shelves had answers for. She liked old maps and broken pottery and any plant that did not belong in Vaelthir. The Ashren tree her father planted outside his office was there because of her, and she loved it all the same.

Her professors marked her down for reading too much into things. Last spring she came to me for a makeup assignment, and I gave her the Book of Flora, which I had set aside for her before she asked. For generations scholars have written in its margins that Flora was a frightened goddess who had to be protected. Ikavelth read it in one afternoon and saw a frightened woman holding on too tightly. She was right, and so far as I know, she was the first.

Professor Thompson has written to the University asking to change a mark.

## Vitharen

Vitharen Undral was a ranger out of the Groves and a physics student at the University. He was the best of his year and he preferred his own company. His thesis said that the stillness of the aetheric fields over Vaelthir was not natural, and that someone was feeding it. The committee agreed his numbers were right and rejected him for believing them. Two of those four signatures belong to men who are now dead or in a cell.

The University will confer his degree with first class honours. Professor Aldric Fenmore, who wrote the one dissenting footnote on that notice, has come back up the south road and asked to be the one who reads it out.

## What I did

For many years I kept a tablet that was not mine to read. It was the Second Tablet of the Book of Flora, the one Stilver never wanted the public to know about, because it would hurt him. I never read it, but I knew who it was meant for when I met her.

I went looking for them at the safehouse and was too late, and found them the next morning at a camp on the road south. I gave Ikavelth the tablet and a letter. I told them to watch out for each other, and that the path would try to take one of them first. I didn't know for sure, but I had a feeling.

On the night of Day 214 the wardens at the Plateau station shot them both in the dark, forty-two rounds for him and thirty-nine for her. Neither of them was armed. Her father had the station's report on his desk the next morning, and he tried to bury it so no one would know that his schemes got his daughter killed.

They were not running. They were walking somewhere, and they very nearly got there. The spring has turned this week. I will not tell you how, but I know it is because of them.

*Edna Thatch is president of the Wexamour Archaeological Society.*
