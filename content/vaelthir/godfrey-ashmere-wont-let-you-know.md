You were told that seven officers were hurt on the station steps. That much was true, but you did not hear the rest. The Chronicle does not care about you, and it does not care about Wexamour. These messages will show you why.

This is an email correspondence between a reporter, Merritt Vance, and the Chronicle's editor, Godfrey Ashmere. In it you will see the editor suppress vital information about the protest and throw it out of the paper for his own gain.

<p>Godfrey,</p>
<p>I am filing the protests piece tonight, and before I do I would like to ask you something. I have heard from more than one person on this desk that their copy is being read by someone other than you before it goes to press. Some of the articles have been killed for no stated reason. Additionally, key details from pieces have gotten out prior to publication: I have heard that one reporter's source, who would only speak in confidence, had the Bureau at her door before the piece ever ran. If that is true, I worry about breaking my promise to my sources and violating their safety. If my copy is going anywhere other than your desk, I would rather know before the next issue goes out.</p>

<p>Godfrey,</p>
<p>Attached. I was on the station steps last night. It was a vigil, it was quiet, and the lantern that burned the officer came from the top of the steps, not from the crowd. I watched it leave the man's hand.</p>
<p>Then the line drew swords on them, Godfrey. I counted thirty dead under the University wall before the Bureau's wagons came for them.</p>
<p>The Bureau took my notebook at the bottom of the steps, so this is written from memory. Where I am not sure, I say so in the copy.</p>
