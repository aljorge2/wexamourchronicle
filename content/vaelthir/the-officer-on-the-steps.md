Officer Aldous Wren is twenty-six years old, and for now he cannot hold a cup on his own. His hands are wrapped in gauze to the wrist, and a dressing runs from under his left ear down into his collar. Twice a day his wife, Mina, sits him at the kitchen table of their narrow house behind the Vaelthir waystation and changes the bandages, while their two daughters, four and six, keep him company. The girls have drawn him get-well cards, and Mina has pinned them along the dresser. In the biggest one he is a knight.

He was posted on the third step of the station the night the crowd was cleared. "We were told to clear the steps, so we cleared them," he said. "It was dark and loud and there were lanterns everywhere. I never saw who threw it. I heard the glass go, and then I was on fire." Two of his fellow officers put him out with their coats. The healers at the waystation infirmary have told him the scars on his neck and jaw will stay, and he is not expected back on duty this season. Mina picked a sliver of painted glass out of his collar that night, and he keeps it in a dish on the windowsill. Two letters are still on it, an I and a P, the end of some word. "To remind me to keep my head up," he said.

He does not hate anyone, he wanted it known. "Most of those people are scared, same as us. I just wish they'd stayed home." His girls ask him every night when he will go back to work. "When it's safe," he tells them. "When the people making it unsafe go home."

*Theodora Lisle is the Chronicle's Vaelthir correspondent. Readers who wish to send Officer Wren and his family their good wishes may leave cards at the front desk of the ORCM Vaelthir station.*
