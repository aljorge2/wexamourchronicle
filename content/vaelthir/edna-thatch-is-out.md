Mrs. Edna Thatch is no longer in the Office's keeping.

She had been held inside the Vaelthir station since the night of the vigil, eighteen days without a charge read to her. At the change of watch on the morning of Day 238, the wardens found her cell open and empty. The lock had been opened with a key, not forced, and the key was back on its hook. The two night wardens were asleep at their table, each beside a cold cup of tea that smelled of honey and something under it.

Nobody was hurt. Nothing was broken.

The Office calls it "an escape with assistance from within." It is questioning every clerk and warden in the building, and it has asked anyone who sees Mrs. Thatch to report her at once. I do not know where she is. If I did, I would not print it.

On the bench she left a sheet of paper with five names on it:

> Harlan Denby.
> Ivy, Sela's girl.
> Alma Thresher.
> Isolde Marsh.
> Wendel Pike.

They are the names she was not given five minutes to read on the station steps. The list is finished now.

A few hours after the cell was found empty, the Office stripped her husband, Magistrate Emory Thatch, of his title, "pending inquiry."
