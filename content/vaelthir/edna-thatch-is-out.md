Mrs. Edna Thatch is no longer in the Office's keeping.

She was taken inside the Vaelthir station on the night of the vigil, Day 219, still holding the list of names she had been reading. For eighteen days she was held there. No charge was read to her that anyone can tell me of. No one from the Society was let in to see her. So far as I can learn, her husband did not visit. The Office had set her before a tribunal on Day 240, and had told this paper, when this paper was still asking, that she was "being held for questioning."

On the night of Day 237 she walked out.

## What the wardens found

I have this from two people who work inside the station and who will not be named, and I have checked what I could of it against the third, who would not speak to me but did not tell me I was wrong.

At the change of watch on the morning of Day 238, the warden coming on found her cell open and empty. The lock had not been forced. It had been opened with a key and left open, and the key was back on its hook in the wardens' room, where it is kept.

The two wardens on the night watch were asleep at their table. Neither of them could say when they had fallen asleep or what they had last seen. By each of them was a cup of tea, gone cold. One of the clerks who was there that morning told me the cups smelled of honey, and of something under the honey that she did not recognize.

Nobody was hurt. Nothing was broken. No one in the station heard a thing.

## What the Office says

The Office calls it "an escape with assistance from within," and that is the first thing it has said in eighteen days that I believe. It has sealed the station, it is questioning every clerk and warden who was in the building that night, and it has added the four clerks already on leave since the protests to the list of persons it wishes to speak to. It has asked anyone who sees Mrs. Thatch to report her at once.

I do not know where Mrs. Thatch is. If I did, I would not print it.

## What she left

On the bench in her cell she left a single sheet of paper. One of the people who told me about the cups saw it before the Bureau took it. It had five names on it, in her hand, and nothing else:

> Harlan Denby.
> Ivy, Sela's girl.
> Alma Thresher.
> Isolde Marsh.
> Wendel Pike.

Those are five of the twenty serpent-folk killed on the strip in the first week of the patrols. They are the five names Mrs. Thatch did not get to read on the station steps.

She had read fourteen when the order came down from the doors, and she was partway into the fifteenth, Harlan Denby, when the line moved. When they took her by the arm she asked to finish, and the officer let her read one more. She chose Hollis Barrow. These are the rest. She was not given three minutes to read them on the steps, so she wrote them down in the cell and left them there for the Office to find.

The list is finished now.

## The morning after

On the morning of Day 238, a few hours after the cell was found empty, the Office removed Magistrate Emory Thatch, her husband, from the magistracy and from his seat on the Office's committee, "pending inquiry." It did not say an inquiry into what. I am told by people inside the Office that it is about his wife and an unlocked door.

I have learned something else about the Magistrate this week. It is about another door, the night of the vigil, and I have given it a page of its own.

*Merritt Vance reports for the Wexamour Archaeological Society. He wrote for the Chronicle's Vaelthir pages until its editor suspended him. If you were held in the Vaelthir station after the vigil and have since been let go, he would like to hear from you, by owl, in confidence.*
