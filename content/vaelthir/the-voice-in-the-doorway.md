On the night of the vigil, I wrote this:

> A voice from the station doors called for the flames to be put out. It did not come from any officer on the steps. I looked, and I could not see who it was in the doorway.

I can see now.

## The book

Every Bureau station keeps an order book. Each night's orders are written into it in the hand of whoever gives them, and signed, so that the officers on the line can say afterward on whose word they acted. The book for the Vaelthir station is kept with the Office's records, on the ground floor, in the rooms the Office cleared the steps to keep safe.

This week someone who works in those rooms showed me the page for Day 219. Not a copy. The page. I am not going to say who, or how, and I would ask the Office not to bother looking, because the people it will look at first had nothing to do with it.

This is the entry, all of it:

> Day 219. Station steps. Clear them. Use what force is required.
> E. Thatch, Magistrate, at the Chair's instruction.

## How I know the hand

When the Bureau came to the Faded Lands Community Board on the ninth, they took the ledger and the letters and every recording the strip had sent in. They left one thing behind: the writ they carried, which the Board was allowed to keep because it had been served on them. The Board lent it to me. It is signed by Magistrate Emory Thatch.

I set the writ beside the page in the order book. The E is the same, closed at the top. The T is crossed long, past the h. The word Magistrate leans the same way and runs out of ink in the same place, at the second a. I have looked at a great many signatures in three years on this desk. These two were written by one man.

There are two E. Thatches in this story. One of them was standing on the steps with a list of the dead, asking for three minutes. The other one wrote "Magistrate" after his name and sent the line down on her.

## Mrs. Thatch

A member of the Society who has seen Mrs. Thatch since she left the station tells me that she knew the voice from the doorway the moment she heard it. I have not been able to ask her myself, and I cannot confirm it, so I am telling you plainly that it was told to me. I put it here because if it is true, then Edna Thatch asked her own husband for three minutes, and he would not give them to her, and she went on reading anyway.

## The four words at the end

The Magistrate wrote the order. He did not write it on his own. It says so, in his hand, at the bottom of the page.

I have followed that instruction as far up as I can, and it does not take long.

**The animals.** In these pages some weeks ago I reported that the serpent-mousers were bought from the budget of the Civil Compliance Initiative. This week the same person inside the Office who helped me then told me the rest of it. The Initiative answers to the Chair alone. Nothing is spent from it without his name on the line. The animals that killed twenty people on the strip in the first week of the patrols, and four more on the square the night of the vigil, were paid for on the authority of Chair Stilver Lugrathe.

**The silence afterward.** The messages taken from Godfrey Ashmere's mailbox and printed here last week are full of the Chair. "Thank you for this, and for the courtesy, which the Chair has noticed." "The Chair would take it as a personal kindness if Mr. Vance were kept off anything touching the Office for the present." "There will be no figure for the square." "The Chair read Miss Lisle's piece on Officer Wren this morning and was moved by it." He did not only allow the thirty to go uncounted. He was kept informed of every step of it, and he was pleased.

**The money.** When the strip asked the Office for relief earlier this season, so its farmers could get through to the next planting, the Office answered with a review and then a finding that the farmers had ruined their own ground. It found no money for them. In the same weeks the Chair found five hundred thousand for a reward, in his own name.

## Who is being blamed

On the morning of Day 238, the Office stripped Emory Thatch of his title and his seat, pending an inquiry that I am told is about his wife's empty cell. The Office is willing to lose a Magistrate over a door left unlocked. It has said nothing about the order he wrote, and it has said nothing at all about the four words at the end of it.

Mrs. Thatch asked for three minutes. The Magistrate gave the order. The Chair gave the Magistrate the instruction. That is as far up as it goes.

*Merritt Vance reports for the Wexamour Archaeological Society. He wrote for the Chronicle's Vaelthir pages until its editor suspended him. The badge numbers of the officers he saw strike people on the night of Day 219 are still in the notebook the Bureau took from him. He has asked for it back. If you hold an order book page, a writ, or anything else written in the hand of a man who gave an order that night, write to him by owl.*
