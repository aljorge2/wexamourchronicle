We owe our readers an explanation for the empty space where a review should be this week, and it is this: Topaz Toepads is in Grove Hospital.

There is no gentle way for a food desk to write the sentence "our food critic has food poisoning," so we will not try to dress it. Late last week Topaz sat down to a meal he intended to review, as he has sat down to a thousand others, and by the small hours he was unwell in a way that a man who eats for a living learns to tell apart from a merely unlucky evening. He made the walk to the townline himself, which the ward tells us he should not have done, and was admitted to Grove Hospital below the wooded slope, where he has been since. We are permitted to tell you that he is stable, that he was never in danger of the serious kind, and that the danger now is chiefly to the composure of the staff, whom he has been reviewing.

Those of you who read this section know the particular unkindness of the timing. This is a man who has made a public principle of eating everything. He accepts no free meal. He stood in the line at Freeman's for the sanguine curd, the reddest dish on the island, and went back a second day for a third helping, and reported it to you without a tremor. He walked the ridge for the honey cake and ate the thing the whole region was fighting over. He has taken into his body, on purpose and for your benefit, every strange and lovely and alarming plate Wexamour has produced this uneasy spring, and it was not any of those that felled him. It was, as best anyone can tell, an ordinary bad kitchen on an ordinary evening, the kind of plain failure that has nothing to do with the season and everything to do with a cook who did not do his job. There is a joke in there about the man who fears nothing on the menu being undone by the one dish he did not think to fear, and Topaz has asked us, by owl, in a hand made shaky by the ward but unmistakably his, not to make it. So we will only gesture at it and move on.

He has, characteristically, declined to tell us where he ate. We pressed. His answer, which we print in full because it is the most reassuring thing about his condition, was this: "I do not name a kitchen until I have decided what I mean about it, and I have not decided, and being poisoned by a place is not a licence to be unfair to it. Give me a few days and my stomach back and I will tell you exactly where I went and precisely what I think, and it will be worse for them than a bad notice because it will be a true one." We take that as proof that the important part of the man is entirely intact.

So there is no review this week, and there may not be one next week, and we would rather tell you why than pretend the desk simply ran quiet. Topaz Toepads will be back at your tables the moment the ward will release him, which by his account cannot come soon enough, and by theirs cannot come soon enough either. Until then we would take it kindly if you sent no dinner invitations and no casseroles. He is, he wants it known, off his food, and he considers this the single greatest professional inconvenience of his life.

*The Vaelthir Desk keeps Topaz Toepads's chair warm and his standards higher. Cards, but not covered dishes, may be sent to the desk by owl and will be carried to the ward.*
