I do not know how to write this one, and I have been writing for these pages a long time.

Posy Hartwell is gone. Most of you already know how, and I am not going to spend her memorial on the water or on the advisory or on any of it, because Posy would have hated that, and because she deserves a page that is about her life and not the last hour of it. She was forty-one. She leaves three children. She leaves the rest of us, too, a whole community that will not find its grief in any official notice.

You knew her voice whether you knew her name or not. If you have ever read a Downs roundup and thought, well, I suppose we could take the little ones to the fern meadows this weekend, that was Posy. She wrote about first broom lessons over the meadows and the morning queue at Grandma Windward's Beastiary and which market stall would let a four-year-old pet the griffin chicks without losing a finger. She once ended a column by asking us to note, for the record, that two of her three children were at that moment outside, and that was the whole of her philosophy right there. Get them out and let them count the tadpoles.

She was, in person, exactly the woman you would hope wrote those columns. Warm without being soft, funny in a dry way that snuck up on you, endlessly patient with everyone's children and slightly less patient with everyone's opinions about her own. We met years ago at the honey cake stall and she looked at me and said, "You are the cake woman, and I am the family woman, so between us we have covered the entire reason anyone reads the Downs pages." She had the sweetest laugh and we were friends from that morning on.

I keep thinking about how much of what she wrote was about safety. She spent years teaching this region's parents how to let their children roam without coming to harm, and there is a cruelty in how she left us. I will only say that nothing about it was her failing. Her advice was time-tested.

What I want to leave you with is Posy-style instruction. She would want the children out this weekend. Not at the lake of course, but in the meadows, at the market, up where the light does that golden thing through the trees. She believed that the small ordinary outings were the point of a life, and that you only had one chance at them. She was right about that the way she was right about most things.

Go outside this weekend and take someone small with you if you can. That is the only tribute she would ever ask for.

*Clementine Ferrow-Aldworth writes about food, family, and life in the Rolling Downs. She was proud to call Posy Hartwell her friend.*
