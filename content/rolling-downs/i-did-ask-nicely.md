Just last week I sat down at this very table and asked the Downs, as gently as I know how, to be kind to one another and to lower its voice. Well, there is now a young officer in Vaelthir with burns to his face and hands, there are seven of them hurt in all, and there is a curfew on the roads. I take no pleasure in being right. I only wish I had been listened to.

I will say I thanked Heaven out loud when I heard the Bureau turned the Millhaven carts around at the Brannock bridge before they ever reached the city. Those are good Downs boys with fields to mind, and I would far rather they were home being cross with me than standing in a crowd that throws lanterns at people. As for the curfew, I cannot see what all the fuss is about. A little quiet for the children never hurt anyone, and if you are home by dusk with your door shut, which is where decent folk have always been at dusk, then you have nothing on earth to fear from it. I would gently suggest our neighbours down on the strip do the same.

So here is my small plea this week. Say a prayer for that officer. He was only standing where he was told to stand. Say one for his family, too, who did not ask for any of this.

*Merrigan Ploughwell farms the middle fields outside Brannock Crossing and writes a regular column for the Rolling Downs pages. She can be reached by owl, or by standing in the lane and hollering, which she does anyway.*
