I woke this morning to a sound I had never heard in my life. It was the blossom coming down, every tree in the lane letting go at once, soft and dry, like a page being turned very slowly. Posey stood in the yard with petals in her hair and asked me if the trees were broken. I told her no. I told her they were finished, which is a different thing, and a good one.

I walked the children down to Brindle before breakfast. The kelpies are home, their manes black again, and there is not one of them but dozens, right up under the footbridge nosing at the children's boots. Nana and Odile always said you see more of them the kinder you are to them.

Nana used to say that a season nailed down is just a death that smells like flowers. This morning it came loose.
