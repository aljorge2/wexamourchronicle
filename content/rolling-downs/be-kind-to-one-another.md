I have been asked, more than once this week, to write something gentle, and I am happy to oblige, because Heaven knows the Downs could use it. We do not all believe the same things this season, and we are not going to, and I think that is perfectly fine. Some of us are grateful the office is finally doing something about whatever has gone wrong down south, and some of us would apparently rather stand on the steps of an ORCM station and shout at clerks who are only doing their jobs. I will not tell anyone what to believe. I will only say that I have watched grown women lecture a Bureau officer outside the Millhaven market as though the poor boy wrote the order himself, and I have read calls for riots in the streets in a season when we can least afford it, and I cannot see what kindness there is in any of it.

So here is my small plea. Be angry if you must, but be angry where it does not frighten the children. All the shouting in Wexamour has not fixed a single field or filled a single shelf, and it will not make the roads one bit safer at night. The office has told us plainly that the patrols are a precaution. If you have nothing to fear on those roads, you have no cause to make a scene about them, and if you do have something to fear, then perhaps you ought to be asking why. Let the people whose job it is get on with keeping us safe and lower your voice. A little kindness would not kill any of us.

*Merrigan Ploughwell farms the middle fields outside Brannock Crossing and writes a regular column for the Rolling Downs pages. She can be reached by owl, or by standing in the lane and hollering, which she does anyway.*
