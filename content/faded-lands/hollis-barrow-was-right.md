Hollis Barrow, 46, wrote for this Board, and he wrote what the rest of us would not. When the whole of the outer reaches agreed never to speak of the south, Hollis went to the maps and the grandfathers and put his name under one plain sentence: *There is something down there.* He was killed past the second creek by the Bureau's animals with his notebook in his hand. The Chronicle declined to print this when he died. We are printing it now, and we are finishing his story for him.

When the south road reopened, eleven of us walked it past the second creek. The old road climbs onto high ground, and at the top is the Plateau Research Station, empty, its doors open and its lamps dark. Beyond it stands a ring of red stones taller than a barn, the "hundreds of Ashren trees" our grandfathers told us about. Several have split open down the middle, and cold water runs out of the cracks. In the dust around the ring were two sets of footprints, side by side and keeping the same pace, that went all the way around the stones and stopped where they started. There was nobody there.

At the second creek on the way home, three women passed us walking north together: the woman in the blue gown from the hollow house, the woman in green from the Downs road, and the tall one with silver hair from the crossing, the ice at her brow dripping. The silver-haired one smiled at the children, a real smile this time, and raised a hand as she went by. We have not seen them since.

There was something down there and maybe there still is. Hollis said so and put his name under it. We are putting ours under his.
