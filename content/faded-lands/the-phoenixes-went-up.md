The phoenixes that left the University square had been roosting for weeks in the dead fields along the south road, thousands of them, on the low farm past the old mile-marker, the Attlebury plot and the Byre place. At the third hour of the night the clocks started, every one of them took the fire at once. It was heard all across Wexamour, from the walls of Vaelthir to the far reaches of the Groves, and the light woke every house on the island. The curfew had the whole strip indoors, and no one was killed. By dawn the dead fields were burned clean to bare soil, with a small warm heap of ash wherever a bird had been. Professor Sabine Orrery of the University aviary counts 6,208 of them, and the old texts say ash from a burning like this may take years to stir.

By noon there were carts on the south road from every corner of Wexamour. The Downs ringed the heaps with the old flood sandbags, with Luna directing from the top of the stack. The Groves wove the vines they had cut from their own doors into windbreaks. University students numbered every heap with a stake. The fairies from the tinker's workshop are threading warmth into the ash, and Mother Bettony has set out a long table to feed whoever comes. A lantern watch now runs the length of the road from dusk to dawn, and green is already coming up between the heaps.

"Every heap is a bird," Professor Orrery said. "We only get to choose whether they are still here when they come back."

*Perrin Callow writes for the Chronicle's Science Desk. The lantern watch rota is pinned to the gate at the low farm past the old mile-marker.*
