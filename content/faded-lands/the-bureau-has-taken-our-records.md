On the evening of the ninth, four Bureau officers came to the Board's room above the Byre granary with a writ issued under the office's new curfew order. They said they were searching for persons sought by the office. There was no one there but the three of us. They did not look for anyone for very long. They took the ledger, the box of letters, and every recording our neighbours have sent us since the patrols came up the south road. That is every stop, every search, every animal loosed, and every account of how our twenty friends died, with the name of the person who wrote it on each one.

We asked you to write it down and tell us. We told you your account would be held in confidence. We meant it, and we are so sorry.

If you sent us an account, you have done nothing wrong. If officers come to your door, be polite and answer only what you are asked. Do not argue the law with them and do not run. Keep your papers on you and keep your children close.

We also wish our neighbours to know that we wrote an obituary for Hollis Barrow, who wrote for this Board and in these pages, and who was killed out past the second creek while taking notes for a story about the animals. We sent it to the Chronicle. The Chronicle has declined to print it.

*Please do not send the Board any more accounts by owl. We can no longer keep them safe.*
