There is a new litter up at the Entmoot conservatory, and I went to see them so you would not have to wait for the telling. Six manticore pups, a cross out of Grandma Windward's old line and a fierce farm cat found in the Rolling Downs, tumbling over one another in the straw with their tails not yet grown into the sting, all ears and outrage and enormous feet. The littlest, a red-pawed thing that Entmoot calls Mantis, fell asleep in the crook of my arm mid-grumble.

Entmoot has been raising manticores for the trail the better part of twenty years, and these are snake-hounds first and last, bred for snakes above all things the way the old houndsmen mean it. A good one, they say, will scent a serpent long before you ever could, and set to hissing at it while you are still admiring the view. Even at eight weeks the pups have it in them. A stranger came up the lane while I was there, only a carter turned round at the wrong gate, and every last one of them lifted its head and went low and quiet until Entmoot called them off.

It is a good season to have one at the door. Entmoot tells me there has never been a list like this year's. Trail-walkers, Groves families, mothers who want one grown alongside the children, and a fair few farmers down on the strip, all of them wanting a pup placed before the autumn comes on. In a season when we have all learned to mind who falls in beside us on the trail and who offers us directions we did not ask for, a hound that will mind it for you is worth its weight and then some.

The pups will be down on the green at the Dellriven market this coming rest-day for anyone who wants to put a word in early, and I defy you to meet Mantis and come away unmoved. Bring the little ones. Just do not, I beg you, bring a snake.
