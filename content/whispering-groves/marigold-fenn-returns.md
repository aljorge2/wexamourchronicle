I wrote the first piece about The Reverie. I want to begin there, because I am not going to stand at the front of this and pretend I arrived clean. I sat in the queue on opening morning and I came away charmed, and I wrote a warm and admiring column, and I told you to go early and go curious and tell me what you found. Some of you did what I said. I have spent the time since trying to make my peace with that sentence, and I have not managed it, and I am beginning to think I am not supposed to.

So here is my second piece, at last, the follow-up I went out to Misthollow to report and did not come back from. It is not the one I meant to file. I cannot give you the inside of that room. I have tried, in the days since they brought me out, to find the words the way I have always found the words, and they are not there, and I have made my peace with that part at least. Everyone who came out talks around it the same way, and I used to think that was because it was too lovely to describe. I know better now. It is not that it cannot be said. It is that saying it does not do it justice and does not do you any good, and I will not spend the one piece I have left on this handing you a door I do not want a single one of you to walk through. Do not go looking for what I saw. That is the last wellness advice I will ever give from these pages, and it is the only true one I ever gave.

What I can give you is what I have instead of the story, which is anger, and I am going to spend it carefully, because I was a reporter before I was a cautionary tale and I intend to be one again.

I have read my own notebook. My family printed it while I was gone, every page, the questions and all. I have read what I wrote before I went in, the lines about following the money before the magic, about who paid for that lodge and who moved the Council to hold the ribbon, about the fact that nobody warm by accident has a closed gate ready for every question. I was right. I want that on the record, not because being right is worth anything to the people who are still not themselves, but because I was right in a notebook on a desk for weeks while the thing I was right about kept its door open, and nobody with a badge picked the notebook up until my sister put it in the newspaper.

Let me tell you what happened while I was inside, as best I have been able to piece it from everyone who was on the outside doing the work. My family looked. A staff reporter at this paper counted names when no office would, and printed them, and kept the list the proprietor would not. Ordinary people on a neighborhood board built a map and a radius and a timeline out of nothing but worry. That is who found the pattern. That is who found us, in the end, or forced the hands of the ones who did. And the whole time, the office whose entire purpose is to manage exactly this closed the premises with a notice, and reviewed, and did not comment on matters under review, and reviewed some more, and moved at the stately, procedural, blameless pace of a body that has decided the worst thing that can happen to it is to be seen moving too fast.

And here is the part I cannot get past, the part I will still be angry about when the rest of this has faded into a thing that once happened to me. By the time anyone official walked through that door to ask Madame Solace a single question, she was not behind it. She was gone. The keeper of a premises under order, closed since the sixth, sought in a review, simply not there when they finally came. You do not slip out of a place that is being watched. She was not being watched. She was being reviewed, which is a different thing, a slower and gentler and more forgiving thing, and she used every day of it, and she is somewhere now that none of us can name, and the office will tell you it does not comment on matters under review.

I am not owed her arrest. I know that. No charge has been filed and I am not going to stand here and pretend to a certainty about her that the facts have not yet earned. But I am owed, and you are owed, and the people still resting and observed at the townline are owed, an honest accounting of how the woman at the center of the only question that mattered was given the time and the quiet to be elsewhere. Nobody kept a list of who went in. Nobody required a permit that would have made her keep one. Nobody moved until the neighbors and a grieving family shamed them into it. And when the moving finally happened, it happened toward the door and not toward the person, and so we have the door, empty and closed and harmless now, and we do not have her.

I owe some thank-yous and I will not bury them at the bottom in small type. To my sister Bryony, who would not sit still, and to my family, who made the paper print my name and my notebook when sitting quietly would have been so much easier. To Merritt Vance, who counted us when it was not fashionable to and kept the list. To the strangers on a watch board who drew a circle on a map around people they had never met. You are the reason I am at this desk and not still behind that door. I know exactly what I owe you, and it is not gratitude of the soft kind, the kind that lets everyone off. It is this. It is the piece. It is refusing to let the story become "they were all found, how nice," when the truer sentence is "they were found, and the one who took them was long gone, because the people whose job it was to hold the door watched it close politely instead."

I am home. I am well enough to be this angry, which the ward tells me is a good sign. I am going to keep writing for these pages, and I am not going to write about wellness anymore, because I have learned what I actually cover, and it is doors, and who holds them, and who is allowed to walk away from one clean while the rest of us are still learning how to sleep. If you know who Madame Solace was before this spring, I am still asking. I will be asking for a long time. Send word to the Whispering Groves desk by owl. I kept the notebook habit. I have a fresh one, and the first page has her name on it, and this time I am not going through any door to fill it.

*Marigold Fenn has returned to the Whispering Groves pages after several weeks unaccounted for. She thanks her family, this newspaper, and the readers who looked for her, and asks that anyone with knowledge of the former proprietor of the Misthollow lodge write to the desk by owl. Correspondence will be held in confidence.*
