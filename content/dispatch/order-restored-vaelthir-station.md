Order has been restored outside the Office of Resource and Civil Management's Vaelthir station after a gathering on its steps grew disorderly last week and was dispersed by officers of the Wexamour Bureau. Eleven persons were detained. The station, which has seen assemblies on its steps most evenings for more than a fortnight, was closed to the public for the remainder of that night and has since resumed normal business.

According to the office, the crowd had grown well beyond the size of previous evenings by nightfall, and a number of those present carried open-flame lanterns up onto the station steps, within a few yards of the record rooms on the ground floor. When asked to put them out, they refused, and officers moved to clear the steps "for the safety of the public, the staff, and the records in the office's keeping."

Seven officers were injured in the course of the dispersal. One suffered burns to his hands, neck, and face when a lit lantern was thrown and broke against him on the steps, and is not expected to return to duty this season. Another was struck from behind and fell the length of the steps, fracturing his wrist. A third was knocked to the ground by a thrown stone and kept overnight at the infirmary with a head wound. A fourth was bitten on the forearm while making an arrest and is being treated for possible venom. The remaining three were treated for sprains, cuts, and heavy bruising that the office described as "consistent with a sustained assault." A small number of persons in the crowd reported minor complaints and were seen at the Vaelthir waystation infirmary as a precaution. All of them were discharged the same night.

Among those detained is Mrs. Edna Thatch, president of the Wexamour Archaeological Society, whom the office has named as the instigator of the gatherings and who is being held for questioning. Mrs. Thatch is the wife of Magistrate Emory Thatch. Neither Mrs. Thatch nor her husband could be reached for comment. Four members of the station's own clerical staff, who left their posts to join a gathering late last month, have been placed on indefinite administrative leave pending review.

"The office has always respected the right of citizens to make their views known," a spokesperson said. "It cannot permit those views to be made known with fire on its doorstep. What happened on those steps was not a vigil. It was a disturbance, and it was handled as one." The spokesperson said a curfew on the square and other affected areas was likely to be put in place in the coming days, and asked residents to keep clear of the station "in a season when this region can least afford disorder."
